What a real nonlinear Pushover curve represents

A Pushover curve relates base shear to the displacement of a control node as an imposed lateral displacement grows. A post-yield response only has engineering meaning when it is computed from a genuinely nonlinear material and structural model. An illustrative curved line is not a capacity calculation.

The published DÈLGHEM example is a 3.0 m tall cantilever of S235 steel, with an IPE 300 section and a fiber-based constitutive model. The OpenSeesPy benchmark completed 32 displacement-controlled increments in the X direction and another 32 in Y. This is a controlled numerical specimen, not a Pushover assessment of the separate eight-storey homepage building.

The actual plastic branch and numerical limits

At the last analysed control displacement of 120 mm, the X path carries approximately 57.37 kN and the Y path 10.11 kN of base shear. The highest recorded steel-fiber plastic strains are approximately 2.05% and 0.86%, respectively. The different flexural axes create different initial stiffnesses and post-yield behavior.

The two curves mark the first observed plastic increment and retain the real numerical points through the end of the analysis. No fictitious descending branch, ultimate strength or collapse displacement has been added. A separate plot tracks the plastic strain against control displacement to make the change in constitutive state visible without overstating its design meaning.

Incremental Dynamic Analysis: intensity and demand

IDA runs transient structural analyses at increasing intensities of a ground-motion input. The DÈLGHEM D2 demonstration uses KratosMultiphysics with Newmark integration and steel-fiber nonlinear elements. Two diagnostic orientations were run at eight discrete scale factors each, ranging from 0.05 to 1.0, producing 16 completed analyses.

At the highest intensity, Strong-X reaches approximately 0.214 g of input PGA and 1.22% peak drift, while Weak-Y reaches approximately 0.031 g and 2.70% peak drift. These are two oriented analytical benchmarks, not independent recorded earthquakes representing a statistical ensemble. No collapse was observed. There is therefore no scientifically justified fragility curve or collapse probability for these cases.

Acceleration histories and structural hysteresis

The time-history charts pair the applied ground acceleration [g] with the calculated control displacement [mm] and recorded base shear. The two input signals contain 721 samples apiece and different time increments; both values are disclosed in the chart. Visitors can select a real computed intensity level instead of watching artificially interpolated runs.

These time histories are analytical diagnostic input signals, not historical ground-motion recordings. An engineering assessment of a particular building requires compatible input selection, realistic mass and damping models, suitable discretization, careful convergence review, and independent model validation.

Frequently asked questions

Are these the capacity curves of the eight-storey model?

No. They belong to an independent S235 IPE 300 vertical cantilever benchmark with X and Y displacement control.

Did the IDA benchmark demonstrate seismic collapse?

No. All 16 diagnostic analyses completed without an observed collapse; no statistical fragility is claimed.

Are these real earthquake records?

No. The inputs are analytical diagnostic acceleration histories actually used by the D2 solvers.
